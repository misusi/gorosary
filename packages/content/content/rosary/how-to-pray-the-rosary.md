## Before you begin

You need very little: a rosary, a quiet corner, and ten or fifteen minutes.
Nothing else is required. If you have no rosary, your fingers will do — one
finger for each small bead is enough to keep count, and the Hail Marys are the
same however they are counted.

Choose a fixed part of the day if you can. The Rosary is not a prayer for
special occasions; it is a habit of the soul, and habits are made by repetition
rather than by enthusiasm. Ten minutes every evening will carry you farther
than an hour once a month.

## The shape of the Rosary

1. The Sign of the Cross.
2. The Act of Contrition (optional, but always worthwhile).
3. One **Our Father**.
4. Three **Hail Marys**.
5. One **Glory Be**.
6. Five decades, each made of one **Our Father**, ten **Hail Marys**, and one **Glory Be**, with a mystery to meditate on.
7. The **Hail, Holy Queen**.
8. A short prayer to close, and the Sign of the Cross.

That is the whole Rosary. Fifty-three Hail Marys, five Our Fathers, five Glory
Bes, one Hail Holy Queen, and the prayers at either end.

## Why the Rosary is shaped this way

- **The fifty Hail Marys** stand for the fifty years of the Blessed Virgin Mary's life. Fifty decades of the angel's words, counted bead by bead, are meant to be a lifetime placed at the service of her Son.
- **The five Our Fathers** are one for each of the five decades. The prayer Christ taught His disciples is said once at the head of every decade, so that each group of ten Hail Marys is set within the Our Father and cannot be said as though it were mere repetition.
- **The five Glory Bes** close the five decades. The Glory Be is built upon the scriptural canticle, and that canticle contains five Glories: the threefold "Holy, Holy, Holy" of Isaiah and of the Apocalypse, the "Worthy is the Lamb" of the Book of Revelation, and the "Amen" with which the doxology of the Epistle of Jude is closed.
- **The three Hail Marys at the beginning** are for the three theological virtues, and the fifty in the decades make fifty-three in all.

## 1. The Sign of the Cross

Touch your forehead, your breast, your left shoulder, your right shoulder,
slowly, while you say:

> In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

The three fingers that touch the forehead name the Trinity; the two that
touch the shoulders cross themselves for the Incarnation and the Redemption.
Make the sign at the beginning of the Rosary, at the end of the Creed, before
a meal, and when you are troubled. It is the shortest prayer and the one that
says the most.

## 2. The Act of Contrition

This step is not part of the Rosary itself, and you may say it or leave it. It
is worth saying. You have just stood before God and asked Him for everything.

> O my God, my heart is sorry for having offended thee. I hate all my sins, because I know how much evil and how great a sin it is to offend thee. I repent of it with my whole heart, and I firmly resolve never to sin again. And I beg of thee, O merciful Lord, by the merits of the passion and death of Jesus Christ, that thou wouldst be merciful to me and forgive me my sins. Amen.

If you prefer the shorter form, this is enough:

> O my God, I am sorry for having offended you, because I know how great a sin it is. I repent of it, and I promise never to sin again. Amen.

## 3. The Our Father

Say the Our Father slowly. This is the prayer Jesus Himself gave, and it is
said once at the beginning of the Rosary and again at the head of every
decade.

> Our Father, who art in heaven, hallowed be Thy name. Thy kingdom come. Thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread. And forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive them that trespass against us. And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Amen.

Do not hurry it. "Give us this day our daily bread" is a small sentence with a
whole life inside it.

## 4. The three Hail Marys

Say the Hail Mary three times. These are the three Hail Marys of the Rosary,
and they are traditionally offered for the three theological virtues: faith,
hope, and charity.

> Hail, Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee. Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus. Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death. Amen.

Say the second and third for faith, hope, and charity. Some people instead
offer them for purity of soul, love of God, and love of neighbour. Either way
they are the same three words of the angel, and they set your heart in the
right place before the decades begin.

## 5. The Glory Be

Say the Glory Be once, on the single bead that follows the crucifix.

> Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit, as it was in the beginning, is now, and shall ever be. Amen. Amen. Amen.

It is said again at the end of each of the five decades.

## 6. The five decades

A decade is ten Hail Marys. Each decade is a meditation on one mystery, and
the set of five you pray depends on the day of the week or the season of the
liturgical year.

A decade is prayed like this:

1. Say the **Our Father** on the large bead.
2. Say the **Glory Be** on the single bead that follows the ten.
3. Say ten **Hail Marys**, one on each of the small beads; on the first of the ten, slow down and meditate on the mystery of the decade.
4. Say the **Glory Be** on the next single bead.
5. Say one more **Hail Mary**, this time with the intention of the mystery you have just prayed, asking for the grace that mystery is meant to give.

Then take the next large bead and begin the second decade in the same way,
and so on through the fifth.

### The beads of a rosary

A rosary has one large bead for the Our Father and small beads for the Hail
Mary, joined by a single bead for the Glory Be. On the usual Dominican
arrangement it works out like this:

1. On the crucifix, the **Sign of the Cross**.
2. On the single bead below the crucifix, the **Glory Be**.
3. On the next three beads, the three **Hail Marys**.
4. On the next single bead, the **Our Father** for the first decade, followed by the **ten Hail Marys** of that decade.
5. On the next single bead, the **Our Father** for the second decade, and the same again for the third, fourth, and fifth.
6. On the last bead, the **Hail, Holy Queen**.

### What to meditate on in each decade

The mysteries of the day are the subject of the five decades. Pray each one
slowly, and ask yourself a single question at each Hail Mary.

**The Joyful Mysteries — Mondays and Saturdays.** The Annunciation, the
Visitation, the Nativity, the Presentation in the Temple, and the Finding in
the Temple. Meditate on the humility of Mary, the charity of her visit, the
poverty of Bethlehem, the offering of all that is most dear to God, and the
child who is found where he belongs.

**The Sorrowful Mysteries — Tuesdays and Fridays.** The Agony in the Garden,
the Scourging at the Pillar, the Crowning with Thorns, the Carrying of the
Cross, and the Crucifixion. Meditate on the Son who obeys, the innocent lamb
who bears the punishment we have earned, the crown that is both mockery and
promise, the road to Calvary, and the death by which the world was saved.

**The Glorious Mysteries — Wednesdays and Sundays.** The Resurrection, the
Ascension, the Coming of the Holy Spirit, the Assumption, and the Coronation
of the Blessed Virgin Mary. Meditate on the empty tomb, the promise of His
return, the fire that came upon the Apostles, the hope of the Assumption,
and the crown given to the handmaid of the Lord.

**The Luminous Mysteries — Thursdays.** The Baptism of the Lord, the Wedding
Feast at Cana, the Proclamation of the Kingdom of God, the Transfiguration,
and the Institution of the Eucharist. Meditate on the Son who takes our
place, the first sign of His power worked at a wedding, the word that opens
the Church and the soul, the light of the mountain, and the gift of Himself
under the appearances of bread and wine.

## 7. The Hail, Holy Queen

After the fifth decade, say the Hail, Holy Queen on the last bead before the
crucifix. Kneel if you are able to.

> Hail, Queen of Heaven, hail, enthroned above all the angels; Salve, O Lady, all who call upon thee; Queen of Mercy, pray for us; and turn not thou from us, but give us to Jesus, thy Beloved Son. O merciful, O loving, O our Lady, intercede for us with the Father.

Some use the *Salve Regina* instead, which is the antiphon of the Blessed
Virgin Mary:

> Salve, Regina, mater misericordiae; vita, dulcedo, et spes nostra, Salve. Ad te clamamus exsules filii Evae; ad te suspiramus gementes et flentes in hac lacrimarum valle. Eia ergo, advocata nostra, illos tuos misericordes oculos ad nos converte. Et Jesum, benedictum fructum ventris tui, nobis post hoc exsilium ostende. O clemens, O pia, O dulcis Virgo Maria.

## 8. The final prayer

Close with a short prayer. Say the words you mean. Something like this,
in your own manner:

> O God, whose mercy is infinite and whose gifts are without price, grant us, by the prayers of the Blessed Virgin Mary and of all the saints, to walk this day in the way of your will, and to love you with all our heart. Amen.

The Rosary is not finished when the last bead is reached. It is finished when
you have made your offering and gone back to your work.

## A word about attention

Praying the Rosary well is not a matter of finishing quickly. A decade prayed
in five minutes with the mind elsewhere is worth less than one decade prayed
slowly with the mystery actually in front of you. When the mind wanders — and
it will — do not stop and do not scold yourself. Say a short Hail Mary for
the distraction, or simply return to the mystery and begin again with the
next Hail Mary.

If you miss a mystery, do not stop; pick it up the next time. If you must
break off, end with the Hail, Holy Queen and a short prayer, and you have
not lost the prayer. The Rosary is a lifetime of prayer, not a performance.
