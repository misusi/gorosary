## The Traditional Form

> I believe in You, my Lord and my God, and I firmly believe all that You have
> revealed and taught us by Your holy Church, through the inspired Word, and
> the Sacraments, and the Saints, and the Church's definitions and decrees. I
> believe that You are the one true God, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and that
> You became man for us in Jesus Christ, and that You suffered under Pontius
> Pilate, and rose again on the third day, and are alive today, and that You
> will come again in glory to judge the living and the dead. I believe that You
> are truly, really, and substantially present in the Blessed Sacrament of the
> Altar, Body, Blood, Soul, and Divinity. I believe in the communion of saints,
> the forgiveness of sins, the resurrection of the body, and the life
> everlasting. Amen.

## The Short Form

> Jesus, I believe in You, for You are the Son of God, the true and eternal God.
> I believe all that Your holy Church teaches. Let this faith increase in me.
> Amen.

## A Note

Faith is a gift, and a gift is asked for as well as given. The short act is
said at the end of the Rosary, before the Blessed Sacrament is exposed, and
before the reading of the Gospel on a Sunday. In the older devotional books it
is placed beside the **Act of Contrition**, the two together making a complete
turning of the heart — sorrow for what has been done against God, and faith in
what God has done for us in Christ.
