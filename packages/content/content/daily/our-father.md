## The Prayer

> Our Father who art in heaven, hallowed be thy name. Thy kingdom come. Thy
> will be done on earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread.
> And forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive them that trespass against us.
> And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Amen.

## The Liturgical Commentary

The Lord's Prayer is the one prayer that is given to us word for word by
Christ Himself. It was spoken to us in this form by Our Lord Jesus Christ, and
the Church has never altered it. The commentary that follows takes it line by
line, as the older devotional books do, so that the prayer may be said slowly
and understood as it is said.

## 1. Our Father who art in heaven

> Our Father who art in heaven.

Here the Lord teaches us to address God as a Father, and not as a king. We
say *our* Father, not *my* Father, because He is the Father of all the just,
and because we are His children together, not one by one. The words *who art
in heaven* lift the heart above the earth, so that the prayer begins where
everything begins, with God.

## 2. Hallowed be thy name

> Hallowed be thy name.

By this we ask that the name of God may be praised in us and in the world, and
that nothing of ours may profane it. It is the first petition of the prayer,
because the sanctification of the name of God is the beginning of all good. To
hallow God's name in ourselves is to live so that our tongue is never raised
in vain and our life is never a contradiction to what we profess.

## 3. Thy kingdom come

> Thy kingdom come.

The kingdom of God is His reign in souls, His Church, His judgment, and His
eternal life. We ask for it to come in us, in the Church, and in the world.
The prayer does not ask that we be taken out of the world, but that the world
be changed, so that the kingdom of God may be in it as certainly as it is in
heaven.

## 4. Thy will be done

> Thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven.

Here we ask that we may do God's will, perfectly, in everything, as the
angels do in heaven. This is the heart of the prayer, and it is said three
times a day in the Mass, immediately after the embolism of the Our Father.

The words *on earth as it is in heaven* bind the two together. Heaven is the
measure, and earth is the place where the measure is to be kept. The prayer
does not ask for a world other than the one we live in, but for this world to
be governed by the same justice, the same mercy, and the same order that reign
in heaven.

## 5. Give us this day our daily bread

> Give us this day our daily bread.

We ask for what we need for the body and for the soul: the food of this day,
and the Bread of Life. We ask it *this day*, and not for tomorrow, so that we
may learn to trust God day by day and never hoard what has been given to us.
The bread asked for is enough for the day; the prayer does not teach us to
beg for more than we need.

## 6. And forgive us our trespasses

> And forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive them that trespass against us.

We ask for forgiveness, and we bind the condition of it to our own. We cannot
ask God to forgive us unless we are willing to forgive others, and the older
books put it plainly: the measure we give is the measure we receive. This
petition is said first at the Mass, before the Eucharistic prayer, because
forgiveness is the condition of the offering.

## 7. Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil

> And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil.

The prayer ends with a plea for protection rather than a plea for anything.
We do not ask to be left alone in the world, but to be held in the hand of
God. The word translated here *evil* is wider than sin: it is everything that
opposes the will of God, whether it is the world, the flesh, or the devil.
The last word of the prayer is *Amen*, which means *so be it*, and which seals
the whole of it with the agreement of the heart.

## The Prayer in the Mass

The Our Father is said by the priest in the middle of the Mass, and by the
people with him, immediately before the embolism:

> Deliver us, O Lord, from every evil, and grant us peace in our day. In Your
> mercy keep us free from all distress, so that, by the help of Your mercy,
> we may be always free from sin, and safely escape all the adversities of
> this passing world. And grant us peace in Your day, in Your mercy, that we
> may be filled with the joy and peace of the Holy Spirit, and that we may
> help those who are joyful, and have mercy on those who are afflicted. Let
> this world, O Lord, be the dwelling place of Your peace and Your sanctuary
> of the elect. And by Your great mercy, spare us, O Lord, from all the
> troubles of our hearts, and from all the dangers of our going and coming.
> Cover us, O Lord, with the shadow of Your protection, and grant that we may
> never fall into sin, nor be surprised by sudden death. But grant us, O Lord,
> that we may always be ready to meet You with joy, so that we may be found
> among those who come before You with their faces sanctified, and in that we
> may inherit Your promises of salvation. Through Jesus Christ our Lord. Amen.

## The Doxology

Some manuscripts of the Gospel, among them the oldest, add at the end of the
Lord's Prayer these words, which the Church has never made part of the
prayer itself:

> For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, now and for ever.
> Amen.

## The Prayer in Scripture

The Lord's Prayer is given twice in the Gospel: by Our Lord in the Sermon on
the Mount, in Matthew chapter six, and again in Luke chapter eleven, where it
is spoken shortly after the disciples ask Him to teach them to pray. In both
places it is preceded by the words *This is how you ought to pray*, and in
both places the petition for the kingdom, the will of God, and daily bread
comes before the request for forgiveness and for protection from evil.

## A Note

The prayer is said slowly. It is short, and there is no reason to hurry over
seven lines. Many who say the Our Father daily for a lifetime are surprised,
on the day they count, to discover that they have never once said *Thy will
be done* with the whole of their heart.
