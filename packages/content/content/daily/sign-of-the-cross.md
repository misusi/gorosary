## What It Is

The Sign of the Cross is the gesture by which a Christian professes faith in
the Trinity and in the redemption of the world by the Cross. It is the first
gesture of the Christian: made in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and
of the Holy Spirit.

There are two parts to it, and both matter. There is the **words**, and there
is the **gesture**. The words are the same in every language; the gesture is
the same in every country.

## The Words

> In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

## The Gesture

Trace the sign upon yourself with the thumb of the right hand:

1. From the **forehead** to the **breast** — the Father, and the Son.
2. From the **left shoulder** to the **right shoulder** — the Holy Spirit.
3. Then with the thumb of the right hand upon the **left shoulder**, and upon the **right shoulder** — the two-fold Cross of the Apostles.

The sign is made slowly, and the words are said slowly with it, so that the
hand and the voice keep the same pace.

## When It Is Made

- At the **beginning of every prayer**, and so at the beginning of the Mass, of the Rosary, and of the Divine Mercy Chaplet.
- **Before and after eating**, before partaking of any food, and before drinking.
- **Before sleeping**, as the last act of the day.
- **On entering a church**, and on leaving it.
- At the **Rosary**, at the beginning and at the end, and at the crossing of each decade.
- Before the **Blessed Sacrament** is exposed, and before receiving Holy Communion.
- At the **grave** of the dead, and at the **crossroads** where a grave cross stands.

## The Traditional Blessing

The Sign of the Cross has never been only a habit. It has been taught, and
guarded, and recommended as a protection. This blessing is traditionally
given in Catholic households and in Catholic schools, and it is traditionally
attributed to St. Cyprian of Carthage; the exact wording varies from book to
book, and it is given here in the form in which it has most commonly been
handed down.

> In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.
>
> When you make the sign of the cross, say the words slowly. When you say the
> words, the mystery of the Holy Trinity is praised.
>
> St. Cyprian said: Make this sign in the beginning of all your actions. When
> you are about to sleep, make this sign, and let it be the last act of the
> day, so that your sleep may be peaceful. And when you wake in the morning,
> make this sign as the first act, and let it be the first thing you do.

## A Note

To make the sign of the cross is to remind oneself, in the smallest possible
gesture, of who is God and what He has done. The hand moves in the shape of
the Cross, and the mouth says the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of
the Holy Spirit. It is the whole of the Christian faith folded into a motion
that takes less time than a breath.
