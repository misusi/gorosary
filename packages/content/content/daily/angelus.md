## When the Angelus Is Said

The Angelus is said three times a day: at **dawn**, at **noon**, and at
**dusk**, in the morning, at midday, and at the close of the day. It is
generally said before the **Mass** or before the **Rosary**, and it is the
traditional prayer of the ringing of the bells.

From **Easter to Pentecost** the **Regina Coeli** is said in its place, because
the Church is then in the joy of the Resurrection.

## The Three Verses

The prayer keeps the shape of the Annunciation. The first verse is the angel's
greeting, the second is the canticle of Mary, and the third is Mary's
acceptance of the will of God. After each verse the same response is said.

> The Angel of the Lord announced it to Mary.

Response:

> Blessed art thou among women, O Mary, for the Lord hath magnified thee.

> And Mary said: Behold the handmaid of the Lord; be it done unto me according
> to Thy word.

Response:

> Blessed art thou among women, O Mary, for the Lord hath magnified thee.

> Then was fulfilled that which was spoken by the angel: Behold, a Virgin shall
> conceive, and bear a Son, and shall call His name Jesus.

Response:

> Blessed art thou among women, O Mary, for the Lord hath magnified thee.

Many households and churches use the fuller traditional response, which the
Church herself uses at the Salve Regina:

> Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus.

## The Hail Mary

Then say one **Hail Mary**:

> Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee. Blessed art thou among
> women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus. Holy Mary, Mother of God,
> pray for us sinners, now, and at the hour of our death. Amen.

## The Closing Prayer

> Jesus and Mary, we beseech You, that we may be blessed with You in the
> Kingdom of Heaven. Amen.

## The Bell

The custom of the **bell** is as old as the custom of the prayer. In country
parishes and in convents, a bell is rung three times a day — at dawn, at noon,
and at dusk — and the people within earshot stop what they are doing and say
the Angelus with it.

The bell is rung a little before the hour, so that those at a distance may
gather and be ready when it is finished. When the priest celebrates Mass, the
bells are rung at the elevation of the Host and again after the Communion; the
same bells that call the faithful to the Angelus call them to the Sacrifice.

The bell, then, is a voice of the Church calling her children to pray, and it
carries to the ears of the whole village the same three words that open every
page of the New Testament: Jesus Christ is Lord.

## The Angelus in Latin

> Ave Maria, gratia plena, Dominus tecum. Benedicta tu in mulieribus, et
> benedictus fructus ventris tui, Jesus.

> Et benedicta tu in mulieribus, et benedictus fructus ventris tui.

> Et ecce ancilla Domini: fiat mihi secundum verbum tuum.

> Et benedicta tu in mulieribus, et benedictus fructus ventris tui.

> Jesu, Maria, oramus te, ut mereamur tecum benedicti esse in regno caelorum.

## A Note

The Angelus is short on purpose. It is meant for the moment in the morning
when the day is still new, in the middle of the day when the work is at its
hardest, and at night when the household gathers in. It is a prayer that can be
made by anyone, at any hour, in any state of life.
