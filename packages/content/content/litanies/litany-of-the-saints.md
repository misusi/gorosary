## The Litany

**V.** God, come to my assistance.

**R.** O Lord, make haste to help me.

**V.** Glory be to the Father and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit.

**R.** As it was in the beginning, is now, and will be forever. Amen.

**V.** Holy Mother of God, pray for us.

**R.** That we may be made worthy of the promises of our Lord Jesus Christ.

And so the invocations follow, one after another, each answered with *pray for
us*, until the whole Church is called by name.

**V.** Let us pray.

**R.** O Lord, we beseech thee, to preserve us from all evils, and to quiet all
the enemies of our souls; and to grant us thy peace.

**V.** Our Father.

**R.** Our Father.

**V.** Hail Mary.

**R.** Hail Mary.

**V.** Deliver us, O Lord, from every evil, and grant us peace in our day.

**R.** O Lord, save us.

**V.** And have mercy on us.

**R.** Amen.

## The Litany of the Saints

The General Litany of the Saints is the oldest litany in the Church and the
one that has been prayed in essentially this form for more than a thousand
years. It is built out of the earliest Christian prayer we know of, the
intercessions of the faithful, gathered up and turned into a single cry: *pray
for us*.

The order of the invocations is a catechism in itself.

- **Mary first.** The Mother of God stands at the head of the Church in heaven
  because she stands at the head of the Church on earth. Every other
  intercession in this litany is an echo of hers.
- **Then Joseph, then the Angels.** The Angelic Host is invoked as a whole,
  and then the three Archangels by name, because the Scriptures single them
  out: Michael who fights, Gabriel who announces, Raphael who heals.
- **Then the ranks of the Church on earth.** Holy Father, Holy Mother, Holy
  Virgin. The Apostles, the Evangelists, the Martyrs, the Confessors, the
  Virgins, the Doctors of the Church, the Popes, the Bishops, the Kings and
  Queens, the Monks and Religious, and all the holy men and women.
- **And at the end, all the holy Servants of God.** Nobody is left out. The
  litany closes not with a small number of famous names but with every soul
  who has ever loved God and tried to do His will.

The point of the ordering is the communion of saints. To pray this litany is
to say, in so many words, that we are not alone. We belong to a Church that
spans heaven and earth, that has members we will never meet and saints we
will only read about, and that is quite capable of praying for us when we
cannot pray for ourselves.

It is also a litany of hope. The martyrs were not comfortable people; the
confessors were not lucky; the virgins were not admired. They were ordinary
people who took the Cross up in their own lives, and they are invoked
because what they did can still be done.

## How to Pray It

1. Make the Sign of the Cross.
2. Say the opening: *God, come to my assistance. O Lord, make haste to help
   me.* and the *Glory Be*.
3. Begin the invocations. Say the invocation made by the leader, and the whole
   company answers together: *pray for us*. Where it is said in a group, this
   call and response is the reason it is said in a group.
4. When the invocations are finished, the leader says *Let us pray*, and the
   response is said by all: *O Lord, we beseech thee, to preserve us from all
   evils, and to quiet all the enemies of our souls; and to grant us thy
   peace.*
5. Then say one **Our Father**, one **Hail Mary**, and the final prayer:
   *Deliver us, O Lord, from every evil, O Lord, and grant us peace in our
   day. O Lord, save us. And have mercy on us. Amen.*

The litany is said in a church at the end of the Office of the Dead, at the
end of a procession, on the feast of All Saints, and at the conclusion of
many another office. It is said at home as well. It is a long prayer, and it
grows on you.

If you say it alone, do not hurry the *pray for us*. That is the whole
petition. Everything else is only the names.
