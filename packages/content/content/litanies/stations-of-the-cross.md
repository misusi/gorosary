      The cross was brought out, heavy and rough, hewn for the shoulders of a strong
      man. Jesus stooped and lifted it, and the weight of the whole world settled on
      Him. He who had made the world now carried it, and He made no complaint. From
      that hour He went forward on the road to Calvary, and He has never once looked
      back.
    prayer: >-
      O Jesus, who took up the Cross and went on, give us the courage to shoulder our
      own burdens. Let nothing in our lives be too heavy to carry for You. Teach us
      to go forward as You went forward. Amen.

    meditation: >-
      The road was rough and the wood grew heavier with every step. Beneath the
      crushing load Jesus fell to the ground, and the soldiers struck Him and dragged
      Him on. Yet He rose again and took up the Cross once more. This first fall is
      a mystery of our weakness and of His mercy, which is greater than our
      weakness.
    prayer: >-
      O Jesus, who fell and rose again, forgive us for the times we have fallen away
      from You. Raise us in our hearts as often as we fall. Let Your grace lift us
      when our own strength fails. Amen.

    meditation: >-
      Mary was drawn out from the crowd by the sight of her son led away to die. She
      came to meet Him on the road, and no word was able to comfort either of them.
      She saw the cross that would crush Him, and she saw the obedience that had
      brought her to this hour. Between them they kept a silence more eloquent than
      speech, and the sword of the prophecy was driven all the way through her
      heart.
    prayer: >-
      O Jesus, as You looked upon Your mother, look upon us in our griefs. Comfort
      those who mourn this day, and give them the peace that only You can give. Let
      us stand with Your mother at the foot of the Cross and never leave it. Amen.
  - n: 5

    meditation: >-
      The soldiers forced a stranger out of the crowd to carry the cross for Jesus.
      Simon of Cyrene had come up from the country, and perhaps only out of
      curiosity, but as he took the weight on his shoulders his life changed
      forever. He had been unwilling; he became the faithful one, and the Church has
      honoured him ever since as a true disciple. What was taken from Jesus by force,
      another man carried freely.
    prayer: >-
      O Jesus, who accepted the help of Simon, make us willing to lend a hand. Give
      us the courage to ask for help and the humility to give it. Use us, as You used
      him, to lighten the cross of another. Amen.

      Jesus received the likeness of His features. This station is the heart of the
      devotion to the Holy Face and the Five Wounds. It is a station of tenderness:
      the mother of God stands near, and a stranger looks at Him and cannot look
      away.
    prayer: >-
      O Jesus, whose face was wiped by a woman in the crowd, look upon us in our own
      times of suffering. Let us dry the tears of the poor and the sorrowful, and
      give them bread. May we recognise Your likeness in every face that meets us on
      the way. Amen.

      The wood had cut into the shoulders of Jesus and His strength was failing. He
      fell a second time, and the soldiers, fearing the journey would never end,
      forced Him onward. Each time He fell He rose again, and the three falls have
      long been read as three times our Lord was overcome: by our weakness, by the
      weight of the world's sin, and by the hardness of our own hearts. The wood was
      heavy; what He carried on it was heavier still.
    prayer: >-
      O Jesus, who rose from the second fall and went on, sustain us when we fall
      again. Let us rise with You, and never grow weary of the way. May we carry the
      cross You have given us to the end. Amen.

      A company of women from Jerusalem stood along the road and wept for Jesus. He
      turned and spoke to them, bidding them weep for themselves and for their
      children. Luke's Gospel records that they were daughters of Jerusalem. Their
      tears were not the tears of the crowd that mocked; these were tears of love
      and of grief, and Jesus met them with gentleness.
    prayer: >-
      O Jesus, who comforted the women of Jerusalem, comfort all who mourn today.
      Give them tears that are not bitter, and a hope that will not fail. Let our
      sorrow turn, as Yours did, into love. Amen.

      be turned about, so that the lighter Jesus could carry it. But it was not the
      wood that was the heaviest thing; He was carrying the sins of the world. He
      rose once more and went on, and the road was short after that.
    prayer: >-
      O Jesus, who fell the third time and still went forward, give us the strength to
      rise. When we are broken, be our strength; when we are weary, be our rest. Let
      nothing stop us from finishing the journey You have begun in us. Amen.

      On the hill the soldiers stripped Jesus of everything: His outer cloak, His

    prayer: >-
      O Jesus, stripped of everything, clothe us with Your grace. Let nothing hold us
      that You have not given. Teach us to be poor in spirit and free of the love of
      worldly goods. Make us rich in the only treasure that lasts. Amen.

    prayer: >-
      O Jesus, nailed to the Cross for us, make us love You as You have loved us.
      Forgive us for the sins that nailed You there, and nail our own hearts to
      Yours. Let us never be ashamed of the Cross, and let it be our only hope. Amen.

      For three hours the sun was darkened, and the whole earth mourned. From the

      His spirit. The graves were opened and the saints of long ago rose from their
      tombs. He truly died, for He was truly man; and He truly rose, for He is truly
      God.
    prayer: >-
      O Jesus, who died on the Cross for us, be our death and our life. When our hour
      comes, let us die in Your grace and rise with You on the last day. Receive our
      last breath into Your hands, as You received Your own. Amen.

      the way through. The dead Christ was wrapped in a clean linen cloth, and the
      sorrow of Mary became the sorrow of every mother who has ever lost a child.
    prayer: >-
      O Jesus, taken down from the Cross and given into the arms of Your mother,
      comfort every mother who mourns. Make us tender with those who grieve, and let
      us lay our lives down as You laid Yours down. Raise us up with You on the last
      day. Amen.
  - n: 14
    title: Jesus is Buried in the Tomb
    meditation: >-
      The body of Jesus was carried to the tomb that Joseph of Arimathea had cut in
      the rock, and the great stone was rolled into place. The soldiers went away, and
      the holy women stayed until the Sabbath was ended. When they came again on the
      first day of the week the tomb was empty, and the angel said, He is not here;
      He has risen. The Way of the Cross ends here, in the silence of the tomb, and
      that silence is only the silence before Easter.
    prayer: >-
      O Jesus, buried in the tomb and risen on the third day, we adore You. Let this
      our journey end in the joy of Your Resurrection, which is the fruit of the
      Cross. May we rest tonight in Your peace, certain that You are risen and that
      You will come again. Amen.

## The Stations of the Cross

The Way of the Cross began in Jerusalem. Christians who walked the Via Dolorosa,
the road from the Mount of Olives to the place of the Crucifixion, traced the
path our Lord Himself had walked, and the Church from the first centuries
marked it out: the place of the condemnation, the place of the scourging, the
place of the Cross. When Jerusalem was lost in 1187, the Franciscans took up
the work of keeping these holy places, and through the **1400s** they began to
build outdoor calvaries in Europe, a little shrine for each halt along the
road, so that those who could not go to the Holy Land might walk the same road
at home. The number of stations varied; some had seven, some as many as
thirty.

In 1586 the Roman Missal set out the **fourteen stations** that have been used
ever since, drawn up by St. Jerome. In **1791**, Pope Pius VI gave the Stations
to the faithful as a devotion to be prayed in the churches, in a decree that
began: "In the spirit of our Lord Jesus Christ, in the love of the Cross, and
in the remission of sins." In 1991, Pope John Paul II began the **Scriptural
Way of the Cross**, a fourteen-station sequence that follows the Gospel text
more closely, which Pope Benedict XVI approved for public celebration in
**2007**. That same year, in the Franciscan Year of the Cross, marking the
seven hundredth anniversary of the consecration of the Basilica of Saint
Francis at Assisi, Pope Benedict XVI did something that had not been done for
a long time: he took up the Cross himself and carried it through all fourteen
stations at the Colosseum, so that the Way ended, as it was always meant to
end, in the Paschal Mystery.

## How to Pray the Stations

1. **Go to the stations.** In a church, walk from one station to the next.
   At home, stand at a table with a candle, or simply sit, and take the
   stations one at a time. The place matters less than the order.
2. **At each station, read the meditation.** Do not hurry it. Read it once
   slowly, and then let the station be before you. Some churches set out the
   fourteen scenes in stone or in wood; if you can see the station before you,
   look at it while you read.
3. **Pray the prayer.** Say it in your own words if the printed words are not
   yours. What matters is that you actually turn to Jesus and ask Him for
   something.
4. **Add a short petition.** One thing you will really pray for, one person
   you will really pray for. Keep it concrete: the sick, the dead, a child, a
   marriage, a job, a sorrow you have not told anyone about.
5. **Keep silence for a moment before you move on.** A minute of silence at
   each station will teach you more than a year of distraction.
6. **End where the Way ends.** When the fourteenth station is done, the
   traditional practice is to say the *Creed*, the *Our Father*, and the
   *Hail Mary*, and then to ask for Our Lady's intercession.

The Stations are prayed on Fridays of Lent, especially on Good Friday, and
they may be prayed at any time. Many a parish prays them together on a Friday
evening during Lent; many a family prays them at home on the Fridays of Lent
and on the Fridays of the year.

Prayed without hurry, the Stations are a meditation on love that was not
returned. Look at the fourteen scenes again at the end of the season, and you
will find that you have been walking the same road with Him.
