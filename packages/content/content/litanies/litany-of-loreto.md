## The Litany

**V.** God, come to my assistance.

**R.** O Lord, make haste to help me.

**V.** Glory be to the Father and to the Son and to the Holy Spirit.

**R.** As it was in the beginning, is now, and will be forever. Amen.

Then follow the thirty-three invocations, each one answered with the words
*pray for us*.

At the end:

**V.** Holy Mother of God, thou shalt not forsake us.

**R.** Pray for us.

**V.** Good and gentle Jesus, have mercy on us.

**R.** May the God of all mercies bless and keep us, and may His mercy look upon
us and save us. Amen.

And then the prayer that has always closed this litany:

> Remember, O Blessed Virgin Mary, as you are the true Mother of God, and the
> dispenser of all graces, that I may have a charitable heart toward God and my
> neighbour. And obtain, most pure Lady, by the all-powerful help of your Son
> Jesus Christ, our Lord, the grace to know and to love him, and to be able to
> serve him worthily in this life and to enjoy him in the next. Amen.

## Why We Pray to Mary

The Church has never asked anyone to pray to Mary as if she were God. She is
the handmaid of the Lord, and everything she is honoured for is given to her
for our sake. But the Church does ask us to pray to her, because the New
Testament itself gives us the example.

- **The seed of the woman.** The first promise of the Messiah comes to us
  through Mary. "And there shall be a conflict between you and the woman, and
  between your offspring and her offspring. He shall crush your head, and you
  shall strike his heel." (Genesis 3:15) The promise of a Redeemer was made to
  a woman, and the Child who came was her Son.
- **The Magnificat.** When Mary visited her cousin Elizabeth, the Holy Spirit
  spoke through her: "My soul doth magnify the Lord, and my spirit rejoices in
  God my Saviour." (Luke 1:46-47) The song of the first Christian worshipper is
  the song of Mary. To pray with Mary is to pray with the Church's own first
  witness to the Gospel.
- **"Whatever you ask in prayer, believing, you will receive."** Our Lord
  Himself said it: "Whatever you ask in prayer, believing, you will receive."
  (Mark 11:24) And a few verses earlier: "For whoever says to me, 'Lord, Lord,'
  will not enter the kingdom of heaven unless he does the will of my Father
  who is in heaven." (Matthew 7:21) Mary does the will of the Father. That is
  why the Church asks her to pray for us, and why it is safe to do so. She
  cannot draw down more than God has promised to give.
- **The fullness of grace.** The angel greeted her as *full of grace*, and the
  Church has never taken that away. The Hail Mary is built from the words of
  the angel and the words of Elizabeth: "Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with
  thee." (Luke 1:28) "Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit
  of thy womb." (Luke 1:42) We ask her to pray for us because the one she
  bore is the only one who can answer.

To Mary the Church also gives the highest title of all. "Hail, Mary, full of
grace, the Lord is with thee. Blessed art thou among women." And so the
response continues: "Blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus." The prayer
ends where the Gospel ends, with the Name of Jesus.

## When the Litany is Said

- **At the end of the Rosary.** This is the oldest habit of all and the reason
  most people know the litany by heart. In the Prayers that follow the five
  decades of the Rosary, the Hail, Holy Queen is followed by the Litany of
  Loreto.
- **At the end of a novena.** Nine days of petition close with the litany and
  a final prayer to the saint. Many a novena is begun with the litany and
  finished with it.
- **In the rosary procession.** In the outdoor procession of the Rosary, the
  litany is said between the decades, in place of the meditation, so that the
  whole Rosary may be said aloud by the whole crowd together.
- **In the churches of the East and the West.** It is said in Latin and in every
  tongue, in Latin America as in Poland, in Ireland as in Vietnam. Where the
  rosary is loved, the litany is not far behind it.
- **As a short prayer of the day.** Many of the faithful say it in the morning
  and at night in place of a longer set of prayers, because it is brief and it
  puts Mary between us and everything that is hard.
- **At the ringing of the Angelus.** Where the custom holds, the litany is said
  with the Angelus at dawn, at noon, and at dusk.

Say it slowly if you can. The point of a litany is not to finish it but to
keep saying, thirty-three times, that you need help.
