## Our Lady of the Snows

The title is very old, and it comes from a story the Romans have never tired of
telling. In the fourth century, in the heat of August, snow fell on the
Esquiline Hill in Rome; the place where it fell was marked out, and a church
was built over it. That church is Santa Maria Maggiore, the oldest of the great
Marian shrines of the world, where the relics of the crib of Our Lord have lain
for fifteen hundred years and where the people of the city have come to pray
ever since.

The feast is kept on the fifth of August. The snow is the symbol: white,
silent, gentle, covering what is ugly without taking it away, and melting into
the ground so that the field bears something afterward.

The devotion has always been associated with protection. A city remembers a
snowfall; a family remembers a fire that did not spread; a village remembers a
river that did not come. Our Lady of the Snows is the patron of those who were
kept safe when they had no right to expect it.

## The Nine Days

The nine prayers below move from the snow itself to the tradition of Rome, to
the basilica, to the city, to trouble, and then to the two scourges that have
destroyed most homes and most villages: fire and flood. The eighth day is for
the exiles, of whom there are very many now.

This novena is traditionally made in the first days of August, and it may be
made at the beginning of a winter or at the beginning of a season of storms.
Make it for a place as well as for a person: for a street, a village, a house,
a parish, a country.

## How to Pray It

Make the Sign of the Cross, say the prayer of the day, then the Rosary of five
decades, and one more decade for the intention of the novena. On the first and
ninth days, fast from bread and water until noon and give alms, that the
shelter of this Lady may be spread a little further.

It is a good custom to begin this novena on the fifth of August and to say it
before a picture of the Madonna of the Snows. Where there is no such picture,
a litany of the saints, or a statue of the Blessed Virgin, will serve.
