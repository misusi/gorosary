## The Saint

Patrick was born in Britain in the fifth century, and was taken captive into
Ireland as a boy. He was a shepherd on the slopes of the Wicklow hills before
he was a bishop. Then a voice came to him in a dream, and he went to the
mainland of Europe to be made ready, and he came back to the island that had
held him captive and told it, in a language it understood, that Christ had
died and risen for it.

He is the apostle of Ireland, and the patron of that country, of missionary work
everywhere, and of the removal of scourge. Tradition has given him a bell, and a
shamrock, and the sign of the cross drawn on the ground with the tip of a
staff, and the snakes driven headlong into the sea. It is also said that when
the alarm was given, three hundred and sixty thousand saints rose out of their
graves and marched beside him into battle. His feast is kept on the seventeenth
of March.

## The Nine Days

These nine prayers are the old Irish novena, the one traditionally made for the
conversion of Ireland and for the lifting of every scourge from it: the scourge
of war, of famine, of pestilence, and of sin. They are also made for any
individual soul. Read them slowly. The seventh day asks for miracles, and the
eighth for the removal of punishment; offer both as you would offer a cup of
water to a thirsty man.

## How to Pray It

The ancient Irish novena is made with the Rosary, and it was traditionally begun
with fasting and ended with alms. Make the Sign of the Cross, say the prayer
of the day, then the five decades of the Rosary, and one more decade for the
intention of the novena. On the first and ninth days, fast from bread and water
until noon, and give something to the poor in the name of St. Patrick.

To the people of Ireland this novena is a homecoming. Say it for a nation that
has been through much, and for the faith that has been carried there through
centuries of hardship, and for those who have lost their way there and do not
know it yet.
