## Our Lady of Perpetual Help

The icon of Our Lady of Perpetual Help is a Byzantine image of the Virgin
holding the Child Christ, painted in the manner of the Cretan school. It was
brought to Rome in the year 1499 and given to the confraternity of the
Shepherds of the Cross, who kept it in the church of Santa Maria in Aracoeli;
and the Church, having been convinced of the many graces received through it,
has never allowed it to be taken down from the altar to which it was devoted.

Two archangels stand beside the throne, Michael and Gabriel, carrying the
instruments of the Passion. Mary is shown between the Cross and the Child,
between the sorrow that is coming and the sorrow that has already been suffered,
and she is the one who holds both. Her face is turned toward the beholder and
her hands are open, which is the whole meaning of the title: the perpetual help
of Christians.

The title was added to the Litany of Loreto by Pope Pius IX in the year 1866.
The Church has entrusted this Mary to the mothers, to the young, and to the
sick, and to all who have no one else to turn to.

## The Nine Days

The nine prayers below begin with the icon itself and move through the things
that are painted on it: the title, the face, the archangels, the Child. Then
they turn to the three for whom this Mary has always been especially entrusted:
mothers, the young, and the sick.

This novena is made for the help of the Church, and it is made for people who
have run out of help. Do not be afraid to bring the whole difficulty. She is
called the perpetual help precisely of people who have nothing left to try.

## How to Pray It

Make the Sign of the Cross, say the prayer of the day, then the Rosary of five
decades, and one more decade for the intention of the novena. On the first and
ninth days, fast from bread and water until noon and give alms, that this help
may reach someone else through us.

The traditional custom is to say this novena before the icon itself, or before
any image of the Virgin. Where there is no image, it is said with the Rosary
in the hands, because the Virgin of Perpetual Help is inseparable from it.
