## The Saint

Joseph was a carpenter of Nazareth, and the Gospel calls him just. When the
angel came to Mary and told her that she would bear the Son of God, Joseph was
not there to see it; he heard of it afterward, in an angel's dream, and he
obeyed without asking to understand. The Child grew up in his house. Joseph
taught Him the trade of a man, and the Lord of the world learned it from him.

He is the Man of Silence, and the silence of Joseph is the silence of one who
has been told what to do and does it. He is the spouse of Mary, the just man,
the worker, the guardian of Jesus, the provider for many, the father of the
Church, and the patron of those who are dying. His feast is kept on the
nineteenth of March.

## The Nine Days

The nine prayers below move outward from the man himself to the work he was
given: the home, the trade, the family, the care of the dying. They are meant to
be said one day at a time, without hurry, and the last of them is a prayer of
thanksgiving rather than of asking.

Where the novena is made in a household, it is a good custom to say it in the
evening, after the day's work is done, with the family present. Joseph's
sanctity was made out of ordinary work, and it is in ordinary work that his
intercession is most needed.

## How to Pray It

Make the Sign of the Cross, say the prayer of the day, and then the Rosary:
five decades for the five mysteries, and one more decade for the intention of
the novena. If you can, go to Mass on the first and last days of the novena,
and go to Confession before you begin, so that the nine days are made in the
grace of God.

St. Joseph is the patron of those who provide for others, and that is very
often a silent office. Pray this novena for the worker, the father, the mother,
the provider, and for those whose work has grown heavy upon them. He knows what
it is to carry a household on his shoulders without ever saying that he is
carrying it.
