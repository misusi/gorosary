## Our Lady, Undoer of Knots

The Greeks call her *Theotokos of the Unbreakable Bond*, and the tradition
that carries her name is the oldest we have. It comes from the ninth century,
from the last years of the Iconoclasts, when a strange doctrine held that the
Mother of God was the author of the Incarnation and not only the mother of
the Son of God. Against that error the seventh Ecumenical Council of 787
settled the matter: the Holy Spirit is the author of the Incarnation, and Mary
is the mother of God. But the older title — the Theotokos who binds and
looses — was not thrown away with the error. It was kept, and it was given a
meaning of its own: she is the one who binds and unbinds the hearts of men
for God.

Her feast was kept in the East on 8 September, and in the West it is kept
among the Marian feasts of September, on the same day as the Nativity of the
Blessed Virgin Mary. To her is attributed the untying of what cannot be
untied by any other means: the knot of a marriage in danger, the knot of a
heart closed against grace, the knot of sin that has become habit.

The paintings of her show her with the Child in her arms and with a cord or a
band in her hand — the cord of her own bond, which holds fast what has been
bound to Christ. She is the one who holds the thread, and who can cut it when
it is a snare.

## The Nine Days

These nine prayers are the old Greek novena, the one made to the Theotokos of
the Unbreakable Bond. It was made in the East for a long time before it was
made in the West, and it is made for anything that is tied and cannot be
untied: a marriage, a habit, a grief, a life that seems to have come
together without God in it. Read the prayers slowly. They are not meant to be
rushed, and the ninth is the one that ties the whole nine days together.

## How to Pray It

Make the Sign of the Cross, say the prayer of the day, and then the Rosary —
the whole of it, with a decade for the intention of the novena. The old
practice was to add to this a litany or a psalm, and to end with a visit to
some place where the Mother of God is honoured.

Some make this novena for a knot they are in the middle of and cannot see the
end of. Some make it before a marriage, or for a marriage that is in danger.
Some make it for a person they love and cannot help. Say it for whatever it
is that will not come untied; that is the whole reason for it.
