## What a Novena Is

A novena is nine days of prayer. The word is Latin for nine: *novem*. It is
made to God, but it is made through someone else: a saint, or a mystery of
the faith such as the Sacred Heart of Jesus or the Holy Spirit. And it is
always made for a particular intention.

The practice is ancient. The Apostles and the early Christians kept a nine-day
fast and prayer before the feast of Pentecost, and the Church has ever kept the
shape of it: nine days, one intention, a set form of prayer, and something given
up to God along with it.

A novena may be made alone, in a family, or by a whole parish together. It may
be made for anything: for a soul, for a marriage, for the dead, for the
healing of the country, for peace, for a vocation. But it is best made for one
intention, and that intention is better still when it is a particular person.

## How the Nine Days Are Counted

The nine days are consecutive, and they are counted inclusively: the day you
begin is the first day, and the ninth day ends the novena. They are not nine
days that begin after a preparation; the preparation is part of them.

Before you begin, settle four things:

1. The intention. Say plainly, in your own words, what you are asking for.
   Write it down. Do not change it during the nine days unless you are given
   light to do so.
2. The saint or the mystery. Choose the novena whose prayers suit the
   intention. Read them once through before you begin.
3. The time and the place. Fix them. The best time is the same time each day,
   and the best place is where you will not be easily interrupted.
4. The beginning. If you can, go to Mass on the first day, and go to
   Confession before you begin, so that you begin the novena in the grace of
   God rather than outside it.

## What to Pray Each Day

The daily prayers of a novena are three:

1. A short invocation: the prayer of the novena itself, said once at the
   beginning and once at the end of the day.
2. The Rosary: all five decades, slowly, with one Hail Mary for each thought
   of the mystery you are contemplating.
3. A decade of the Rosary: one more decade, offered entirely for the
   intention of the novena.

Begin and end with the Sign of the Cross. Where the novena provides a
different prayer for each day, use the prayer of that day, and do not run ahead:
the order of the nine days is part of the shape of the devotion.

## The Fast and the Alms

Two days of the novena are set apart:

1. The first day is a day of fasting. Fast from bread and water until noon,
   as the Church fasts on the two days of Ember and on the vigils of the
   great feasts.
2. The ninth day is a day of almsgiving. Give something to the poor, and give
   it because of the intention of the novena. Bread, money, and time are all
   acceptable; the poor are the point of it.
3. The ninth day should also be closed with Holy Mass and, where it is possible,
   Holy Communion.

Where a person cannot fast without harm to health, the fast is exchanged for
an act of penance: an extra prayer, an hour of silence, a service given to
somebody who has none coming to them. Nothing is lost that is given in place
of it.

## If the Novena Is Interrupted

A novena is broken by a day on which the prayers are not said at all, and by
some things even when they are said: by a failure of charity, by drunkenness,
by any deliberate sin against God. It is not broken by illness, by a death in
the family, by bad weather, by the noise of the house, or by illness in the
middle of the prayers.

If the novena is broken, begin again from the first day. This is the traditional
rule and it is the rule the Church has always followed. You do not make up a
lost day by saying two days together, and you do not skip ahead. You begin
again, with the same intention if it is the same intention, and with a new
intention if God has given you a new one.

Some people are tempted to stop entirely. Do not. The whole point of a novena
is that it can be begun again.

## A Prayer for the Intention

Say this once at the beginning, and again at the end of the ninth day. Fill in
the two blanks before you begin, and do not fill them in again.

> O God, our Father, source of every good gift, we thank you for these nine
> days of prayer. Look upon your servant, and look upon the intention we place
> before you: [state your intention here]. By the intercession of [name the saint
> or mystery to whom the novena is addressed], and by the grace of our Lord
> Jesus Christ, who lives and reigns with you, God our Father, in the unity of
> the Holy Spirit, for ever and ever. Amen.

## A Form to Copy

Write this out, or copy it into the front of a book, and fill it in before you
begin:

1. The intention: [__________]
2. The saint or mystery: [__________]
3. The time of day: [__________]
4. The place: [__________]
5. The first day, with Mass and Confession if possible: [__________]
6. The ninth day, with Mass, Communion, and alms: [__________]

There is no better way to make a novena than this one, and no better way of
beginning it than to say the prayer above, and then to begin.
