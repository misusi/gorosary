## The Holy Spirit

The Holy Spirit is God, the third Person of the Trinity, who proceeds from the
Father and the Son and is equal to them in glory. He spoke to the prophets and
he spoke through the Apostles; he is the one who came upon the disciples in the
upper room, breathing on them, so that they might receive power to witness. The
Church has never thought of the Spirit as a power we can switch on. He is a
person, and he is a guest, and a guest must be asked for.

Scripture names his gifts: wisdom, understanding, counsel, fortitude, knowledge,
piety, and the fear of the Lord. It names his fruits as well: charity, joy,
peace, patience, benignity, goodness, longanimity, mildness, faith, modesty,
continence, and chastity. The gifts are given to us as we need them; the fruits
are what grows in us when we have made the gift of ourselves.

## The Nine Days

The first day is the classic prayer for the grace of the Holy Spirit, which
the Church has made its own and which may be recited before any task. From
there the novena moves through the gifts, the renewal of the heart, comfort,
strength, love, and the fruits, and closes with the promise of the Father.

The traditional novena to the Holy Spirit is made on the Eve of Pentecost and
through the nine days following, so that it ends on the feast itself. Made at
any other time, it is a novena of preparation for whatever the Holy Spirit
wants to do next in your life.

## How to Pray It

Make the Sign of the Cross, and say the prayer of the day with a real
petition, not a formal one. Then the Rosary: five decades for the five
mysteries, and one more decade for the intention of the novena. On the first
and ninth days, fast from bread and water until noon and give alms, that the
Spirit who is "the giver of every good gift" may be asked for with the right
heart.

Above all, be ready to be changed. This novena is not a way of getting more
out of yourself. It is a way of letting go of yourself, so that the Spirit can
have room to work, and so that the fruits of the Spirit can be seen in the
ordinary places where you live.
