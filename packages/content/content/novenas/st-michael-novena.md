## The Saint

Michael is called the archangel, which in the Greek means the chief of the
angels, and Scripture has given him a place in three great scenes: standing
before God with the other angels, guarding the way of the tree of life at the
gate of Eden, and fighting the dragon in the Apocalypse, where the word
"Michael" is used for the commander of the armies of heaven.

He is the patron of the Church, of the faithful, of the departed, and of those
who defend others. The Church has given him to us as a shield. His feast is
kept on the twenty-ninth of September, and the day following is the feast of
St. Gabriel, his fellow archangel. Our Lady of Perpetual Help is painted with
him at her side, carrying the lance and the nails and the cross, so that the
Passion of Christ may never be looked upon without the memory of the war that
was fought for us.

## The Nine Days

The nine prayers below move from the soldier to the guardian, to the friend of
the Church, to the comforter, and finally to the one who receives the soul at
its journey's end. They are meant for the Church in its present distress and
for anyone who is afraid.

This novena is also made for the dead, particularly for those who died in
sudden or violent ways, and for anyone who is troubled about the fate of a
soul in the hour of its passing.

## How to Pray It

Make the Sign of the Cross, say the prayer of the day, then the Rosary of five
decades, and one more decade for the intention of the novena. Where the scapular
of St. Michael is worn, it should be worn on these nine days with the intention
of the novena. On the first and ninth days, fast from bread and water until noon.

Pray this novena when you are afraid, and especially when you are afraid for
somebody else. The name of Michael means "who is like God", and that is the
whole of what we ask of him: that the likeness of God may be seen in what
happens to us.
