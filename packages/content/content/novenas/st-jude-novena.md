## The Saint

Jude was one of the twelve apostles, and the brother of Simon the Zealot and of
James. The Apostles' Creed that the Church has received bears his name among the
twelve, and tradition has long joined his name with two of the prayers the
Church has handed down: the Our Father, which came from the Apostles, and the
Hail Mary, which was given back to them. Novenas to St. Jude have therefore
always been made with the Rosary at their heart.

He is called the patron of desperate and difficult cases and of those who are in
great need. The world did not want him; they would have taken him for a thief,
because the Gospel names a Judas among the men who were closest to Jesus. Jude
saw what that meant for himself, and chose to carry his faith where it would be
mocked. Because he stood in the place where no one else would stand, he became
the helper of those who have no helper. The Church keeps his feast on the
twenty-eighth of October.

## The Nine Days

Each day below has its own prayer, and the nine are meant to be said in order
over nine days. The prayers grow: the first asks for his intercession, the
middle days ask for his help in matters of faith and of grace, and the ninth
gives thanks and leaves the matter in his hands. Do not read them all at once.
A novena said in a single sitting has the form of a novena but not the spirit
of it.

## How to Pray It

Begin with the Sign of the Cross. Say the prayer of the day slowly, and mean
it. Then the Rosary: five decades, with the mystery of the day of the Rosary
held in mind for each decade, and one more decade offered for the intention of
the novena. Finish with the prayer of the day a second time, and with a short
Act of Contrition for the sins that have kept you from praying as you should.

If a day is missed, begin the novena again from the first day. There is no
hurry; there is only the matter of praying it all.

These nine days are made for hard things: a marriage that is not being
resolved, a soul that has gone astray, a body that is not healing, a name that
has been blackened. Bring such things to St. Jude, and do not let go of them.
He is the patron of hopeless cases, and hope is his office.
