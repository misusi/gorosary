## What the App Shows Today

The Calendar section shows the commemoration for the current day: the saints
whose feast falls on today's date, and the great days of the season. When the
day is a Sunday or a solemnity, that day comes first, and the saints of the
date are given beneath it, because on a solemnity the whole Church is busy
with the solemnity.

## What a Memorial Is

A memorial is a day on which the Church makes a saint present in the Eucharist
of the day. It is not a feast: the Church does not gather for it as for a
feast, but the Mass is offered for the saint and his name is mentioned in the
prayers of the Mass.

In the General Roman Calendar the memorials are of two kinds. **Obligatory
memorials** are kept by the whole Church. **Optional memorials** are proposed
and are celebrated where the local calendar, or the freedom of the day, allows.
An optional memorial is usually passed over entirely when a feast falls on the
same day, and is only remembered when a solemnity does.

## The General Roman Calendar and National Calendars

There is one calendar for the whole Church, the General Roman Calendar, and
beside it there are the national and diocesan calendars.

A **national calendar** may add saints of local importance and may change the
rank of a commemoration from optional to obligatory. The calendars of the
United States, of Ireland, of England and Wales, and of Poland each keep
saints and observances of their own. A **diocesan calendar** may add a few
more still, and may also move a commemoration to a different day.

Where a national or diocesan calendar differs from the General Roman
Calendar, the local calendar is followed in that country. Everywhere else the
General Roman Calendar stands.

## Why a Saint May Appear on More Than One Date

- **A saint may have more than one feast.** St. Nicholas is commemorated on
  6 December, the day of his death; the translation of his relics to Bari on
  9 May is kept in some calendars.
- **A saint may be kept under two names.** St. Thérèse of Lisieux is also
  called St. Thérèse of the Child Jesus, and is the same saint.
- **A feast may have been moved.** Where a day of greater rank has taken the
  place of a saint's day, the saint may be commemorated on the nearest free
  day instead.
- **Two different saints may share a name,** so that one date carries two
  names, and each is honoured on his own day.

## How to Find a Saint by Name

Use the search box and type the name as you know it, whether that is
*Kateri*, *Teresa of Avila*, or *John Bosco*. The search looks through the
title and the body of every prayer and every commemoration in the app, so a
saint may be found in a novena as easily as in the calendar.

If you know the month and the day but not the name, the **Saints of the
Year** gives every fixed-date commemoration of the calendar in order, from
the first of January to the thirty-first of December. And if you want to know
why a particular saint was placed where he was, the calendar is where that
story begins.
