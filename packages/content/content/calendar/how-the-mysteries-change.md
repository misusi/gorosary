## There are four sets of mysteries

The Rosary meditates on twenty mysteries — five sets of five — but only one set
is prayed at a time, and which one changes over the year.

- The **Joyful Mysteries**: the Annunciation, the Visitation, the Nativity, the
  Presentation, and the Finding in the Temple. They begin at the Annunciation
  and run through the finding of the Child in the Temple.
- The **Sorrowful Mysteries**: the Agony in the Garden, the Scourging, the
  Crowning with Thorns, the Carrying of the Cross, and the Crucifixion. They
  begin at the Lord in the garden and end on the Cross.
- The **Glorious Mysteries**: the Resurrection, the Ascension, the Coming of
  the Holy Spirit, the Assumption, and the Coronation of the Blessed Virgin
  Mary. They begin at the empty tomb and end with Mary in glory.
- The **Luminous Mysteries**: the Baptism of the Lord, the Wedding Feast at
  Cana, the Proclamation of the Kingdom, the Transfiguration, and the
  Institution of the Eucharist. They are not part of the Rosary proper, and are
  traditionally prayed on Thursdays.

## The liturgical season

The set that is prayed follows the season of the Church's year.

- From the **First Sunday of Advent** until **Lent**, the **Joyful** Mysteries
  are prayed.
- From **Ash Wednesday** until **Easter Sunday**, the **Sorrowful** Mysteries
  are prayed.
- From **Easter Sunday** until **Pentecost**, the **Glorious** Mysteries are
  prayed.
- From **Pentecost** until Advent begins again, the **Joyful** Mysteries are
  prayed.

This is why the rosary you are saying changes as the year turns. The Church
prays with the life of Christ in the order that Christ lived it, and the
liturgical year is a year-long meditation on that life.

## The feasts that override the season

Certain feasts carry their own set of mysteries, whatever the season is doing.

- **15 September** — the Feast of Our Lady of Sorrows. The **Sorrowful**
  Mysteries, because the day is given to Mary's grief.
- **7 October** — the Feast of Our Lady of the Rosary. The **Joyful**
  Mysteries.
- **15 August** — the Feast of the Assumption. The **Joyful** Mysteries.
- **8 December** — the Feast of the Immaculate Conception. The **Joyful**
  Mysteries.
- **1 January** — the Solemnity of Mary, Mother of God. The **Joyful**
  Mysteries.
- **All Saints**, 1 November. The **Joyful** Mysteries.
- **All Souls**, 2 November. The Rosary is traditionally not said on this day,
  so the **Glorious** Mysteries are prayed in its place.
- **Corpus Christi** and the **Sacred Heart**, which follow it. The **Sorrowful**
  Mysteries, because both feasts are given to the Passion.

## The older weekly cycle

Long before the current calendar, the set was chosen by the day of the week:
Joyful on Mondays and Saturdays, Sorrowful on Tuesdays and Fridays, Glorious on
Wednesdays and Sundays, and Luminous on Thursdays. Many people still pray it
this way, and it is a perfectly good discipline.

This is why GoRosary shows both. The mystery set at the top of the page follows
the liturgical year, because that is the Church's own reckoning. Underneath it
is the answer the older weekly cycle would give for today. Pray whichever you
are used to.

## Which is right?

Both. The season is the deeper tradition and the one the Church observes; the
weekly cycle is the older custom and the one most people grew up with. Nothing
about either is wrong. What matters is that you know which one you are saying,
and that you say the whole Rosary.

## How Easter is worked out

The dates this page depends on are not fixed. Easter is the first Sunday after
the first full moon on or after the vernal equinox, and from Easter every
movable feast of the year follows: Ash Wednesday forty-six days before it,
Palm Sunday a week before it, Ascension thirty-nine days after it, Pentecost
forty-nine days after it, Corpus Christi sixty days after it.

GoRosary calculates this rather than storing it, so the rosary on any date in
any year is right without anyone updating a table.
